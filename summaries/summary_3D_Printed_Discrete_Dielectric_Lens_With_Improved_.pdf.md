Based on the extracted technical details provided from the paper, here is the structured summary:

### 1. Core Method: What did they do?
* **Design & Fabrication:** The authors designed and fabricated a 3D-printed discrete dielectric lens operating in the 50–110 GHz frequency range using stereolithography additive manufacturing (utilizing Gray resin with $\epsilon_r = 2.738$ and $\tan\delta = 0.01$ at a period of $p = 1.6\text{ mm}$).
* **Matching Techniques:** To reduce medium-to-air reflections, they implemented multi-section matching transformers using binomial and Chebyshev configurations consisting of two or three layers of pins (instead of traditional slots).

### 2. Main Conclusions: What were the key dosimetry results?
* *Note:* As indicated in the extracted text, the paper focuses exclusively on antenna engineering and electromagnetic performance (lens gain and bandwidth) rather than biological interactions or safety standards. Therefore, **there are no non-ionizing radiation dosimetry results** in this study. 
* **Electromagnetic Performance (Key Findings):** 
  * The multi-section matching transformers vastly outperform unmatched lenses and conventional quarter-wave transformers.
  * Unit cells achieved fractional bandwidths ($\text{BW}_{-20\text{dB}}$) of 42.5% and 79.5% for two- and three-layer binomial transformers, and up to 83.45% and 88.8% for Chebyshev transformers, respectively.
  * Simulations demonstrated a maximum realized lens gain of up to 28.93 dBi at 105 GHz for the two-section design and 29.58 dBi at 107.5 GHz for the three-section design.
