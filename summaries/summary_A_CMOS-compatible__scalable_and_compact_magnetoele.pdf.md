Based on the provided technical details from the paper, here is the structured summary:

### 1. Core Method: What did they do?
* **Device Integration:** Developed a CMOS-compatible magnetoelectric (ME) spin-torque microwave detector that integrates a 200 µm diameter ME antenna with magnetic tunnel junctions (MTJs) for direct electromagnetic-to-DC conversion.
* **Mechanism & Modeling:** Utilized strain-mediated magnetoelectric coupling (SMMC) to achieve microwave detection. Modeled the device's nonlinear rectification response using the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert (LLG) equation—incorporating specific magnetostriction coefficients ($B_1$ and $B_2$) and elastic stiffness parameters—to quantify how AC strain modulates magnetic anisotropy and enhances sensitivity.

### 2. Main Conclusions: What were the key dosimetry results?
* **High Sensitivity:** Achieved an ultrahigh detection sensitivity exceeding 90 kV/W (approaching 91.9 kV/W at 19.3 nW) at resonance, which further scaled up to 446 kV/W when using an array of four MTJs.
* **Low Power & Noise Operation:** Successfully operated at sub-microwatt/nanowire input power levels (reaching up to 24.5 nW) with a low noise-equivalent power (NEP) of approximately $3 \times 10^{-12}\text{ W}/\sqrt{\text{Hz}}$ (3 pW/√Hz).
* **Compact Footprint:** Demonstrated an ultra-compact spatial design with a total area occupancy of less than $0.4\text{ mm}^2$.
