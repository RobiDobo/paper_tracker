### 1. Core Method: What did they do?
* **Simulations:** Performed full-wave electromagnetic simulations using CST Microwave Studio (employing open/PML boundaries and adaptive meshing) to model the fundamental transverse electric mode ($\text{TE}_{01\delta}$) of MCT and ZST dielectric resonator antennas (DRAs).
* **Experimental/System Setup:** Implemented a wireless cryogenic-to-room-temperature sensing link utilizing a (Zr,Sn)TiO₄ (ZST) dielectric resonator antenna operated at 10 K, driven with a low input power of 1 mW (0 dBm) to prevent radiative heating.
* **Sensing Demonstration:** Conducted dual-modality sensing of a dielectric target via near-field frequency shifts and far-field amplitude modulations.

### 2. Main Conclusions: What were the key dosimetry and electromagnetic results?
* **Field Enhancement:** Quantified $H$-field enhancement factors of ~33 for MCT at 3.38 GHz and ~26 for ZST at 2.52 GHz.
* **Antenna Performance:** The ZST resonator achieved near-critical coupling ($\beta \approx 0.835$), a reflection coefficient ($S_{11}$) of $-20.88\text{ dB}$, a peak directivity of 4.27 dBi, and a 5.54 front-to-back ratio. 
* **Radiation Pattern & Thermal Management:** ZST exhibited a forward-biased radiation pattern aligned with the window normal, proving structurally superior for minimizing radiative heating and establishing efficient cryogenic links compared to the quasi-omnidirectional MCT resonator.
* **Sensing Reach:** The system successfully detected a dielectric target via near-field frequency shifts (up to $-2.5\text{ MHz}$ within one wavelength) and far-field amplitude modulations detectable up to $4\lambda$ (~46 cm).
