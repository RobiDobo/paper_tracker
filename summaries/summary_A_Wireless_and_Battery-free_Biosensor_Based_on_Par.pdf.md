### 1. Core Method
The researchers developed a **passive, battery-free LC resonator-based biosensor** designed for wireless, non-ionizing microwave spectroscopy. The core innovation lies in the **geometric configuration of the sensor lines**:
*   **Symmetric current flow:** Used to push the dominant electric field outside the sensor structure, maximizing sensitivity to changes in external permittivity (glucose concentration).
*   **Opposite current flow:** Used to increase internal capacitance, maximizing sensitivity to physical deformation (mechanical pressure/displacement).
*   **Validation:** The system employs parallel-plate capacitance modeling and Gauss’s law, with dosimetric validation performed by injecting sensors into phantom muscle tissue at a 2 mm depth.

### 2. Main Conclusions
The study confirmed that passive LC resonators are viable, biocompatible alternatives for both wearable and implantable biosensing. Key performance metrics include:
*   **Glucose Sensitivity:** 500 kHz/(mg/dL), suitable for minimally invasive applications.
*   **Pressure Sensitivity:** 2.5×10³ MHz/mm, suitable for wound monitoring applications.
*   **Interference Management:** The authors successfully implemented a noise-rejection equation to calibrate signal interference, ensuring reliable data extraction from implanted sensors in lossy media (tissue).
*   **Versatility:** The sensors prove that configuration-specific current flow can tune a single platform to prioritize either chemical sensing (glucose) or physical sensing (pressure).
