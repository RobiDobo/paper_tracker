### 1. Core Method
The researchers developed and characterized a **multi-chroic kinetic inductance detector (KID) array** optimized for broadband astronomical observations ranging from 125 to 365 GHz. The design utilizes **hierarchical phased-array antennas** to capture electromagnetic signals. To assess detector performance and sensitivity, the team employed dosimetry-related methodologies, specifically monitoring quasiparticle generation-recombination noise and optical efficiency through blackbody temperature sweeps and the analysis of cosmic-ray interaction events.

### 2. Main Conclusions
*   **Performance Validation:** The measured performance metrics, specifically **Noise Equivalent Power (NEP)** and **quasiparticle lifetimes**, showed strong alignment with theoretical **Mattis-Bardeen predictions**.
*   **Technical Efficacy:** The study successfully confirmed that the hierarchical phased-array antenna design is effective for multi-chroic sensing, demonstrating that the detector array maintains reliable sensitivity across the specified broadband spectrum.
*   **Characterization:** The use of cosmic-ray event analysis proved to be a robust method for characterizing the baseline noise and stability of the KID array in a realistic observational environment.
