Based on the technical details provided, here is the structured summary of the paper:

### 1. Core Method
The researchers developed a cryogenic (300 mK) near-field scanning microwave microscope (NSMM) designed for non-invasive nanoscale imaging of quantum systems. The system integrates a miniaturized superconducting fractal resonator with an atomic force microscope (AFM) tip. To achieve high-precision measurements at extremely low power levels (-100 dBm, or ~1000 photons), the authors implemented a **Pound-Drever-Hall (PDH) frequency-locking scheme**. This method stabilizes the microwave resonator against phase and thermal fluctuations, allowing for the mapping of microwave impedance and dissipation at the sub-atto-farad scale.

### 2. Main Conclusions
*   **High Sensitivity:** The instrument achieves a capacitive sensitivity of $6.4 \times 10^{-20}$ F/$\sqrt{\text{Hz}}$ (also reported as $0.2\text{--}0.38$ aF/$\sqrt{\text{Hz}}$).
*   **Performance Benchmark:** This performance level is four orders of magnitude superior to conventional scanning capacitance microscopes.
*   **Non-Invasiveness:** By operating at exceptionally low power, the system minimizes thermal photon-induced quasiparticle generation, enabling the study of sensitive superconducting samples without inducing decoherence.

### 3. Limitations / Future Work
*   **Primary Noise Source:** The authors identify **mechanical vibrational noise** as the current limiting factor for the instrument's sensitivity.
*   **Scope:** The provided text clarifies that the research is focused entirely on instrumental sensitivity for physics/materials research and does not address biological dosimetry or applications. 
*   *Note:* While not explicitly detailed in the provided snippets, the gap implies that future work would likely involve mechanical dampening or vibration isolation improvements to further push the capacitive noise floor below the current $0.2$ zF/$\sqrt{\text{Hz}}$ limit.
