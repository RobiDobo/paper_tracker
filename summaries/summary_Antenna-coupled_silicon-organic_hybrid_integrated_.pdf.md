### 1. Core Method
The researchers developed a compact, integrated electromagnetic field sensor using a **Silicon-Organic Hybrid (SOH)** platform. The device architecture consists of a **gold bowtie antenna** coupled to an **electro-optic (EO) polymer-refilled silicon slot photonic crystal waveguide (PCW)**. 
*   **Mechanism:** The bowtie antenna captures incident electromagnetic radiation (ranging from GHz to THz frequencies) and concentrates it into the slot waveguide, where the "slow-light" effect significantly enhances the interaction between the RF field and the EO polymer. 
*   **Detection:** This field concentration induces a refractive index change in the polymer, which modulates an optical signal. This allows for high-sensitivity, non-perturbative field measurement without the need for external power supplies or conductive probes.

### 2. Main Conclusions
The integration of optimized poling and slow-light enhancement produced significant performance metrics for dosimetry and field sensing:
*   **Sensitivity:** Achieved a record-high sensitivity of **0.000027 V/m·Hz⁻¹/²**.
*   **Modulation Efficiency:** Demonstrated an effective electro-optic coefficient ($r_{33}$) of **1230 pm/V**.
*   **Bandwidth:** The sensor exhibits a broad operational range, with measurements spanning from **1 GHz to 10 THz**, and a demonstrated 3-dB modulation bandwidth of up to **11 GHz**.
*   **Field Enhancement:** The gold bowtie antenna provides a maximum field enhancement factor of approximately **10,000**, allowing for the detection of low-intensity electromagnetic fields (minimum detectable field of 2.5 V/m at 8.4 GHz).
*   **Non-invasiveness:** By using optical detection rather than conductive leads, the sensor minimizes field perturbation, making it ideal for accurate dosimetry applications.
