### 1. Core Method
The authors utilize a **circuit-theoretic framework** to model mutual coupling (MC) within super-wideband (SW) uniform linear arrays. This framework is used to derive closed-form expressions for the average received signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) under phase-controlled (PC) beamforming. The analysis specifically investigates how tight electromagnetic coupling between antenna elements affects frequency-dependent phase shifts and beam squinting.

### 2. Main Conclusions
*   **Mitigation of Beam Squint:** High mutual coupling in tightly coupled SW arrays acts as a natural mechanism to mitigate beam squinting effects.
*   **Bandwidth Extension:** Unlike conventional weakly coupled arrays, tightly coupled arrays demonstrate nonlinear, frequency-dependent phase characteristics that allow PC beamformers to operate across significantly wider bandwidths without substantial SNR degradation.
*   **System Simplification:** By reducing the frequency dependence of the channel, strong mutual coupling reduces the necessity for complex, power-hungry wideband time-delay compensation hardware.
