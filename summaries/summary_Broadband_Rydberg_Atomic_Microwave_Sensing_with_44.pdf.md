### 1. Core Method: What did they do?
* **Experimental Setup:** Developed a broadband Rydberg atomic microwave sensing scheme using a thermal $^{87}\text{Rb}$ vapor cell, utilizing a five-level atomic energy scheme based on electromagnetically induced transparency (EIT) and Autler-Townes splitting (ATs).
* **Modulation Technique:** Introduced an auxiliary microwave field (coupled to Rydberg states $|r_1\rangle$ and $|r_3\rangle$) with a tunable Rabi frequency ($\Omega_A$) and frequency detuning ($\Delta_A$) to finely modulate the Rydberg energy levels via AC Stark shifts.
* **Theoretical Framework:** Analyzed the system using the Lindblad master equation and Floquet theory under a superheterodyne detection framework to broaden the instantaneous bandwidth without sacrificing sensitivity.

### 2. Main Conclusions: What were the key dosimetry results?
* **Instantaneous Bandwidth (IB):** Significantly broadened to **44.6 MHz** ($\pm 22.3\text{ MHz}$) at a local microwave frequency of 16.03 GHz (achieved at an auxiliary Rabi frequency of $\Omega_A/2\pi = 51\text{ MHz}$ and detuning of $\Delta_A/2\pi = -0.72\text{ GHz}$).
* **Electric Field Sensitivity:** Successfully maintained a high sensitivity of **$225.7\text{ nV}\cdot\text{cm}^{-1}\cdot\text{Hz}^{-1/2}$** concurrently with the broadband operation.

### 3. Limitations / Future Work: What are the gaps mentioned by the authors?
* **Sensitivity Degradation:** The study noted that system sensitivity degradation is directly linked to the AC Stark shift induced by the auxiliary microwave field on the Rydberg states, highlighting a trade-off that future iterations will need to continue optimizing. *(Note: The provided text focuses primarily on these mechanisms and results, while noting that the broader bibliographic context supports SI-traceable electric field metrology).*
