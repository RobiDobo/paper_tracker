Based on the provided technical details from the paper, here is the final structured summary:

### 1. Core Method: What did they do?
The researchers designed, simulated, and measured a conformal $4\times4$ series-fed patch antenna array operating at a resonant frequency around $58.8\text{ GHz}$ ($58.5\text{–}60\text{ GHz}$ band) tailored for wearable millimeter-wave applications (such as smartwatches, bracelets, and rings). To evaluate the effects of conformal flexing, they analyzed the radiation characteristics (including realized gain, half-power beamwidth [HPBW], and 3D patterns) of the antenna array across a planar structure and various cylindrical bending radii ($R = 12\text{ mm}$, $8\text{ mm}$, and $6\text{ mm}$).

### 2. Main Conclusions: What were the key dosimetry and performance results?
* **Performance Impact of Bending:** As the array was subjected to tighter curvature (from planar down to $R = 6\text{ mm}$), the measured realized gain dropped from $17.2\text{ dB}$ to $11.4\text{ dB}$, while the HPBW widened substantially from $20^\circ$ to $79^\circ$. 
* **Coverage Advantage:** The results demonstrated that bending predictably reduces forward gain while significantly expanding the beamwidth. This characteristic can be strategically leveraged to achieve wide-angle or $360^\circ$ wireless coverage for wearable devices.
* **Power and Safety Context:** The design operates under strict regulatory frameworks constrained by a maximum transmit power of $10\text{ dBm}$ and a maximum EIRP of $40\text{ dBm}$ at $60\text{ GHz}$, requiring high antenna gain to overcome high free-space path losses ($\sim93.85\text{ dB}$ at $20\text{ m}$) while remaining compliant with human body exposure limits.
