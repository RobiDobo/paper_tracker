# Summary for Detection_of_Surface_Waves_During_Femtosecond_Fila.pdf

Based on the technical details provided, here is the structured summary of the study:

### 1. Core Method
The researchers characterized the broadband radio-frequency (RF) emissions—specifically surface waves (SWs)—generated by femtosecond laser-induced plasma filaments. 
*   **Experimental Setup:** An 800 nm, 50 fs, 50 mJ Ti:Sapphire laser (10 Hz) was used to create plasma filaments in atmospheric air.
*   **Measurement:** Near-field electric field measurements were captured using a Prodyn FD-5C2 D-dot probe (2–40 GHz bandwidth) at varying radial ($0.25–5.0$ cm) and longitudinal positions.
*   **Analysis:** Signals were processed via Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) and validated by comparing radial profiles against **Hankel function** models and longitudinal attenuation against **Sommerfeld-Goubau analytic predictions** and **axisymmetric Drude simulations**.

### 2. Main Conclusions
The study confirmed that laser filamentation plasmas act as waveguides for surface plasmon polaritons (SPPs). Key quantitative findings include:
*   **Plasma Parameters:** The plasma column exhibited an effective electron density ($n_e$) of approximately $3 \times 10^{22} \text{ m}^{-3}$ with a radius of $100–500$ µm.
*   **Field Characteristics:** A surface wave electric field amplitude ($E_0$) of $\sim 10^4$ V/m was observed. The radial profile matched a Hankel function with an outer length scale ($r_{router}$) of $\sim 1.0$ cm.
*   **Propagation & Conductivity:** The longitudinal $1/e$ attenuation length ($L_{decay}$) was measured at $4.7$ cm (at 10 GHz), yielding a calculated plasma DC conductivity ($\sigma_{DC}$) of $\sim 170$ S/m. 
*   **Agreement:** The experimental data showed excellent agreement with theoretical models ($R^2 \approx 0.99$), confirming the presence of a longitudinal frequency drift in SPP peak amplitudes.

### 3. Limitations / Future Work
*   **Probe Calibration:** The study explicitly noted that the D-dot probe used for the measurements was uncalibrated, which limits the absolute precision of the detected electric field amplitudes.
*   **Scope of Documentation:** The provided text indicates that the bibliography and sections regarding non-ionizing radiation exposure assessment (in the context of safety dosimetry) were either missing or contained no relevant methodology, suggesting that the study focused strictly on the physics of plasma wave propagation rather than human exposure safety standards.
