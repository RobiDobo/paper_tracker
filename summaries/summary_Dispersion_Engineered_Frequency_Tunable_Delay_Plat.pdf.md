### 1. Core Method
The researchers developed and characterized a high-performance, frequency-tunable delay platform using **Magnetostatic Surface Wave (MSSW)** technology in 18 μm thick **Yttrium Iron Garnet (YIG)** waveguides. The methodology involved:
*   **Dispersion Engineering:** Tailoring the spin wave dispersion by varying the meander-line transducer pitch ($p = 70 \ \mu\text{m}$ and $210 \ \mu\text{m}$) to manipulate group delay and frequency response.
*   **Magnetic Tuning:** Applying varying magnetic bias fields (1500–6500 G) to achieve continuous frequency tuning across the C, X, and Ku bands (6–19.6 GHz).
*   **Performance Benchmarking:** Extracting key metrics—such as unit-time propagation loss, insertion loss, group delay, and propagation Q-factor ($Q_{prop}$)—and analyzing energy dissipation through effective modal relaxation time and Gilbert damping parameters.

### 2. Main Conclusions
The study concludes that the MSSW-YIG platform significantly outperforms state-of-the-art acoustic and photonic delay lines, particularly in high-frequency regimes:
*   **Performance Metrics:** The devices achieved continuous frequency tuning from 6 to 19.6 GHz, group delays of 3.3 to 42.8 ns, and insertion losses between 2.5 and 10.1 dB. 
*   **Superiority:** The platform demonstrated propagation Q-factors up to 4893 and unit-time propagation losses as low as ~55 dB/μs. Notably, the devices exhibited up to 100x lower insertion loss (linear power) compared to acoustic competitors.
*   **Frequency Scaling:** Unlike acoustic platforms constrained by $f \cdot Q$ product limitations, the MSSW-YIG platform maintains a rising $Q_{prop}$ trend as frequency increases, making it a highly effective, frequency-agile RF component.
