# Summary for Exposure_Assessment_for_Wearable_Patch_Antenna_Arr.pdf

1. **Core Method**  
   The study used FDTD simulations (Sim4Life) plus analytical plane-wave modeling to estimate absorbed power density \(S_{ab}\) in skin exposed to wearable patch antennas at **28 GHz** and **39 GHz**. \(S_{ab}\) was averaged over **1 cm²** and **4 cm²** and evaluated in four skin models of increasing complexity: homogeneous dermis; 2-layer; 3-layer; and 4-layer models including stratum corneum, dermis, fat, and muscle.

2. **Main Conclusions**  
   - The **homogeneous skin model underestimated peak \(S_{ab}\) in the stratum corneum** compared with layered models by **14–21% in simulations** and **~30–35% analytically**.
   - Differences among the **layered models themselves were negligible (<9%)**.
   - Penetration depth decreased with increasing frequency, leading to **significantly higher inner-tissue exposure at 28 GHz than at 39 GHz**.
   - Despite these differences, **all scenarios remained below the 20 W/m² limit**.
