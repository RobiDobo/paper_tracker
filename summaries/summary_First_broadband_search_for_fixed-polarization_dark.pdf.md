Based on the technical details provided, here is the structured summary of the GigaBREAD experiment paper:

### 1. Core Method
The authors conducted a broadband search for fixed-polarization dark photon dark matter in the 10.7–12.5 GHz (44–52 $\mu$eV) mass range. Their methodology centers on a **polarization-matched likelihood framework** that accounts for the Earth’s rotation. By utilizing a sidereal harmonic expansion of geometric factors—which models how a fixed dark photon polarization vector aligns with the detector’s sensitivity axis over time—the researchers created precise signal templates. They validated these templates using both Bayesian marginalization and frequentist profiling, calibrated against empirical noise distributions generated via Monte Carlo simulations.

### 2. Main Conclusions
*   **Sensitivity Improvement:** The polarization-aware approach achieved a **20% improvement** in sensitivity for the kinetic mixing parameter ($\chi$) compared to previous studies that assumed random or isotropic polarization.
*   **Parameter Limits:** The experiment established 90% confidence-level upper limits on the kinetic mixing parameter of approximately **$\chi \sim 10^{-12}$**.
*   **Signal Detection:** No statistically significant signal was identified. The global Bayesian evidence ($k_{0}^{global} = -2.5$) fell below the required threshold for a detection candidate, indicating that the observed data remains consistent with the null hypothesis.

### 3. Limitations / Future Work
*   **Scope:** The current analysis was restricted to the 10.7–12.5 GHz frequency band; while the paper establishes a robust framework, it does not address potential signals outside this specific mass range.
*   **Generalizability:** While the study successfully incorporated Earth’s rotation and detector geometry (zenith vs. horizontal alignment), future work could potentially expand this likelihood framework to account for more complex detector configurations or different types of dark matter candidates that exhibit similar time-dependent modulation.
*   **Sensitivity Gaps:** Although the 20% improvement is significant, the experiment remains constrained by the baseline sensitivity of the existing hardware; future efforts would likely involve scaling the detector volume or sensitivity to probe deeper into the $\chi$ parameter space.
