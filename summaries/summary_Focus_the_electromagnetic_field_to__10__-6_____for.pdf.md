### 1. Core Method: What did they do?
* **Antenna Design:** Designed a hybrid silver nanowire-bowtie antenna to capture far-field microwaves and couple them directly into confined electron oscillations within the nanowire.
* **Sensing and Mapping:** Utilized nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond (both ensembles and single centers) as non-invasive quantum probes to detect and map the localized microwave fields.
* **Optical/Magnetic Techniques:** Employed charge state depletion (CSD) nanoscopy, optically detected magnetic resonance (ODMR), and a combination of home-built confocal and wide-field microscopy to measure spatial magnetic field distributions and Rabi oscillations at low excitation powers (14 µW to 174 µW).

### 2. Main Conclusions: What were the key dosimetry results?
* **Extreme Subwavelength Field Localization:** The near-field microwave was successfully confined to a cross-sectional width of $291 \pm 10\text{ nm}$, achieving an extreme subwavelength scale of $2.8 \times 10^{-6}\lambda$ (at $\lambda \approx 10.4\text{ cm}$).
* **Field Spatial Distribution:** The spatial distribution of the magnetic field closely followed a reciprocal function dictated by the Biot-Savart law surrounding the nanowire.
* **Massive Field Enhancement:** The hybrid antenna amplified the local microwave field intensity by a factor of $2.0 \times 10^8$ compared to unassisted far-field excitation.
* **Spin-Interaction Boost:** The microwave-spin interaction strength—quantified via the Rabi oscillation frequency—increased by a factor of $1.4 \times 10^4$, achieving an NV center Rabi frequency of approximately $1.6\text{ }\mu\text{s}^{-1}$ even at a low excitation power of 14 µW, thereby drastically improving nanoscale electromagnetic field sensing and spatial resolution.
