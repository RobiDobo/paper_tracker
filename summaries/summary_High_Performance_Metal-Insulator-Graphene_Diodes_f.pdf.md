Based on the technical details provided, here is the structured summary of the paper:

### 1. Core Method
The researchers characterized **Metal-Insulator-Graphene (MIG) diodes** for radio frequency (RF) power detection. They employed a **Vector Network Analyzer (VNA)** to deliver calibrated RF power across a broad frequency range (2 GHz to 50 GHz). A **bias tee** was utilized for DC biasing, and the device's performance was evaluated by measuring the DC output signal generated via RF rectification.

### 2. Main Conclusions
The MIG diodes demonstrated high performance as scalable RF power detectors, with the following key figures of merit:
*   **Responsivity:** 2.8 V/W at 2.4 GHz and 1.1 V/W at 49.4 GHz.
*   **Nonlinearity:** A maximum power detection responsivity of 26 V⁻¹.
*   **Current Density:** An on-current density of 7.5 A/cm².
*   **Applicability:** The device is confirmed as an effective high-performance detector for non-ionizing RF radiation and potential sensing applications.

### 3. Limitations / Future Work
*   **Scope of Application:** The provided text explicitly notes that the current research focuses on RF power detection and sensing applications rather than **clinical dosimetry**.
*   **Gap:** While the results establish the device as a high-performance detector, the provided information does not detail clinical validation, nor does it explicitly state future work, implying that further research would be required to transition these diodes from sensing/detection applications into clinical dosimetry environments.
