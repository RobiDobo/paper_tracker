1. **Core Method**: 
   The researchers fabricated hybrid polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF)-based nanofibers embedded with multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNT) or graphene nanosheets (GNS) using a one-step electrospinning technique (at 15 kV, 1.2 mL/h). They developed a hybrid triboelectric and piezoelectric nanogenerator (TENG/PENG) operating in a contact-separation mode. Structural and crystallographic properties were evaluated using Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM), X-ray diffraction (XRD), and Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR), while the mechanical-to-electrical conversion performance was tested using a cyclic compression system applying 10 N force at 2 Hz.

2. **Main Conclusions**: 
   *(Note: The provided text does not contain non-ionizing radiation dosimetry results; instead, it focuses on electromechanical energy harvesting performance.)* 
   The incorporation of CNTs and GNS successfully promoted the electroactive $\beta$-phase transition and dipole alignment in PVDF fibers, achieving peak $\beta$-phase fractions of 80.5% (at 5 wt% CNT) and 85.3% (at 2.25 wt% GNS), compared to 59.7% for pristine PVDF. This structural enhancement yielded maximum power densities of 757.9 mW/m² (for CNT-doped) and 1132.8 mW/m² (for GNS-doped devices), successfully driving low-power electronics like LEDs and a stopwatch without external batteries while maintaining mechanical durability over 8,000 compression cycles.
