### 1. Core Method: What did they do?
* **Experimental Setup:** Utilized an upgraded adiabatic dry coaxial microcalorimeter operating across 0.05 – 40 GHz, featuring enhanced thermal stability (fluctuations reduced to ~3 mK) through active/passive shielding and Peltier/PID controls.
* **Calibration & Modeling:** Performed effective efficiency ($\eta_e$) calibration of a 2.92 mm thermoelectric power sensor by evaluating and comparing two specific correction models: the rigorous short-circuit technique (Model 2) and a faster S-parameter/loss-rate method (Model 4).
* **Uncertainty Analysis:** Conducted detailed uncertainty budget analyses for thermoelectric power standards up to 40 GHz, calculating specific variable values, sensitivity coefficients, and uncertainty contributions.

### 2. Main Conclusions: What were the key dosimetry results?
* **Model Performance:** The rigorous short-circuit technique (Model 2) achieved significantly lower measurement uncertainties across all tested frequencies (e.g., at 0.05 GHz, $\eta_e = 0.9923 \pm 0.00049$; at 1 GHz, $\eta_e = 0.9806 \pm 0.00048$; at 10 GHz, $\eta_e = 0.9383 \pm 0.00044$) compared to Model 4.
* **Limitations of Alternative Models:** Model 4 suffered from substantially higher uncertainties (ranging from $0.0054$ to $0.0057$) primarily due to high sensitivities in determining feeding-line losses ($S_{12}$).
* **Validation:** The results successfully validated the official correction model currently applied to the INRIM coaxial microcalorimeter, proving it to be a reliable method for effective efficiency calibrations of thermoelectric standards in the RF and microwave frequency range.
