### 1. Core Method: What did they do?
* **Analytical & Numerical Framework:** Developed a rigorous numerical method combining hyper-singular integral equations (based on graphene surface-impedance boundary conditions) with a Nystrom-type collocation method utilizing Gauss-Chebyshev quadratures to account for kernel singularities and edge behaviors.
* **Conductivity Model:** Employed the Kubo formula to characterize graphene conductivity, incorporating both intra- and interband contributions at terahertz (THz) frequencies.
* **Simulation Scope:** Evaluated microsize graphene strips ($d = 5$ to $60\ \mu\text{m}$), finite and infinite coplanar gratings, relaxation times ($\tau = 0.25$ to $1\ \text{ps}$), chemical potentials ($\mu_c = 0.0$ to $0.39\text{ eV}$), and operational frequencies from $0.1$ to $10\ \text{THz}$ at $T = 300\ \text{K}$ to analyze plane-wave scattering, total scattering cross-sections (TSCS), absorption cross-sections (ACS), and near-field patterns.

### 2. Main Conclusions: What were the key results?
* **Polarization-Dependent Behavior:** H-polarization wave scattering exhibits distinct localized surface plasmon resonances and Fabry-Perot-like standing waves, whereas E-polarization yields no plasmon resonances but produces pronounced Rayleigh anomalies.
* **Tunability & Resonance:** Larger relaxation times increase surface plasmon resonances and wave transmittance by reducing dissipation losses, while higher chemical potentials up-shift resonance frequencies. 
* **Material Characteristics:** Graphene behaves predominantly as a frequency-independent resistor with significant inductive reactance.
* **Applications:** The ability to modulate losses and optimize surface plasmon propagation via chemical potential and relaxation time enables potential applications in tunable THz filters and absorbers.
