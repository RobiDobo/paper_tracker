# Summary for Inverse_Design_of_Broadband_Antennas_for_Terahertz.pdf

**Important scope note:** This paper contains no dosimetry content, so I'll report what the excerpt actually supports rather than invent dosimetry results.

**1. Core Method:** Inverse/procedural design of a broadband THz antenna using simulated annealing, simulated in MATLAB Antenna Toolbox and validated in CST Studio Suite. The optimized polygonal antenna (0.2–1.8 THz, 1215 × 1215 µm² footprint) was conjugate-matched to a 1 kΩ load (1 µm feed gap, gold as PEC, mesh edge ≥ λ_min/8), and benchmarked against a bow-tie reference.

**2. Main Conclusions:** *No dosimetry results exist* — no SAR, power density, field strength, exposure limits, or tissue parameters are reported. The actual results are electrical: power-transfer efficiency e_r = 1 − |Γ|² improved from 0.52 to 0.73 (~40%); near-constant real impedance ~400 Ω vs. ~245 Ω (fluctuating) for the bow-tie; near-perfect match at 283 GHz (Z_a ≈ Z_d, Γ minimized). These bear only indirectly on dosimetry (delivered vs. reflected power), and no absorbed-power density, temperature rise, or exposure assessment appears.

**3. Limitations / Future Work:** None are stated by the authors in the excerpt (only references, with no dosimetry-related citations). Inferred gaps, flagged as such: dosimetric assessment would require the simulated near/far-field distributions, which are not reported here.
