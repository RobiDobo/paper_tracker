### 1. Core Method
The study conducted a comparative performance evaluation of **24 GHz, 60 GHz, and 120 GHz FMCW (Frequency-Modulated Continuous Wave) radar systems** for non-contact vital sign monitoring. The methodology included:
*   **Phantom Testing:** Using metallic and gelatin-based phantoms to simulate cardiopulmonary displacement, with **laser vibrometry** serving as the ground truth.
*   **Human Trials:** Validating heart rate (HR) and respiration rate (RR) against **Polar H10 ECG and accelerometer sensors**.
*   **Analysis:** Assessing displacement estimation resolution and accuracy based on the phase-frequency relationship inherent in the different carrier frequencies.

### 2. Main Conclusions
*   **Performance Scaling:** Higher carrier frequencies and larger bandwidths directly correlate with improved displacement estimation resolution and accuracy.
*   **Superiority of 120 GHz:** The 120 GHz system outperformed the 24 GHz and 60 GHz systems, achieving the lowest baseline noise (0.01 mm) and the highest range accuracy (0.04 ± 0.0 cm at 60 cm).
*   **Clinical Accuracy:** In human trials, the 120 GHz system demonstrated high clinical viability, achieving a mean absolute error of 0.4 bpm for heart rate monitoring.
*   **Feasibility:** Low-power mmWave radar was confirmed as a high-precision, non-contact alternative to traditional, contact-based monitoring methods.
*   **Dosimetry Results:** The provided text explicitly states that there is **no extractable information** regarding exposure assessment, tissue modeling, or safety metrics.
