Based on the extracted technical details from the paper, here is the final structured summary:

### 1. Core Method: What did they do?
The researchers established and evaluated an optimal frequency domain measurement setup using a spectrum analyzer. This configuration was designed to accurately record non-continuous WiFi exposure levels (specifically OFDM signals transmitted in bursts) during various data traffic download scenarios, avoiding the need for additional empirical weighting factors or causing signal overestimation. The evaluation tested multiple analyzer parameters, including a center frequency ($f_c$) of 2412 MHz, a 20 MHz span, an RBW of 0.3 MHz, a VBW of 1 MHz, RMS detection, 501 sweep points, and sweep times of 2.5 ms and 10 ms.

### 2. Main Conclusions: What were the key dosimetry results?
Through statistical analysis (ANOVA) and measured power percentiles, the study concluded that a sweep time (SWT) of 2.5 ms, when paired with an RMS detector and 501 sweep points, is the optimal frequency domain configuration. This specific setup successfully captures realistic and accurate human exposure levels to WiFi radiation from bursty, non-continuous signals.
