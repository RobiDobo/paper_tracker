### 1. Core Method
The study utilizes **X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) depth profiling** to evaluate the effectiveness of **OTS (octadecyltrichlorosilane) passivation** on internal cavity surfaces of MEMS vapor cells. By comparing OTS-coated substrates against bare glass and silicon, the researchers analyzed the reduction of cesium (Cs) adsorption. They employed a **low-temperature bonding scheme** during cell fabrication to ensure the integrity of the OTS coating, followed by **Rydberg spectroscopy** to measure the resulting background electric fields and spectral linewidths.

### 2. Main Conclusions
*   **Surface Adsorption:** OTS coating provides a greater than ten-fold reduction in cesium adsorption compared to untreated surfaces.
*   **Field Mitigation:** The reduction in Cs coverage leads to a significant decrease in background electric fields (measured at **<10 mV/cm**), effectively minimizing surface-induced Stark shifts.
*   **Sensor Precision:** The technique enables stable Rydberg spectroscopy with high-precision spectral linewidths of approximately **300 kHz**, confirming that low-temperature fabrication successfully preserves the passivation layer for quantum sensing applications.
