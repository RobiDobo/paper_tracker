### 1. Core Method: What did they do?
* **Design & Fabrication:** Developed a 28 GHz dual-polarized omnidirectional antenna module using a Megtron7 substrate (0.25 mm thick) and CNC-machined ABS plastic lenses.
* **H-Pol Antenna Structure:** Utilized four compact, capacitively coupled dipole elements fed via slot transmission lines and baluns (converting microstrip to slot TEM modes) to form an equivalent electric current loop.
* **Dual-Polarization & Isolation:** Integrated vertically and horizontally polarized elements, optimizing their spatial separation (horizontal distance $dx \approx 10\text{ mm}$ and vertical separation $dz \approx 22\text{ mm}$) to mitigate mutual interference.
* **Evaluation:** Employed full-wave electromagnetic simulations and experimental characterization in an anechoic chamber.

### 2. Main Conclusions: What were the key dosimetry/performance results?
* **Impedance Bandwidth:** Achieved a measured $-10\text{ dB}$ impedance bandwidth of 2.2 GHz (ranging from 24 to over 32 GHz) with overlapping bandwidths around the 28 GHz central frequency.
* **Isolation & Polarization:** Demonstrated high isolation exceeding 32 dB between polarizations, along with a measured co-to-cross polarization ratio of approximately 22 dB.
* **Gain & Uniformity:** The horizontally polarized (H-pol) reference antenna achieved a measured realized gain of 1.35 dBi. Furthermore, both polarizations exhibited low horizontal plane gain variations (less than 0.6 dB for H-pol, and limited to 1.14 dB and 0.7 dB overall), confirming the design's high omnidirectional stability and suitability for millimeter-wave wireless channel and handset testing.
