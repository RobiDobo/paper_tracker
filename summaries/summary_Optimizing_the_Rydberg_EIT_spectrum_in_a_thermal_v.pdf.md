Based on the provided technical details from the paper, here is the structured summary:

### 1. Core Method: What did they do?
* **Experimental Setup:** Utilized a ladder-type electromagnetically-induced-transparency (EIT) configuration in a thermal $^{87}\text{Rb}$ vapor cell (temperatures ranging from $27^\circ\text{C}$ to $65^\circ\text{C}$, optimized at $51^\circ\text{C}$) using counter-propagating 780-nm probe and 480-nm coupling laser beams.
* **Modeling & Simulation:** Developed an analytic transmission model using optical Bloch equations that incorporates Clebsch-Gordan coefficients, Zeeman splittings, and Doppler shifts.
* **Optimization:** Investigated and optimized the Rydberg-EIT spectra by tuning laser polarizations, magnetic fields (ranging from 4 to 22 Gauss), optical densities, and laser intensities (optimum probe intensity around $0.04–0.044\text{ W/cm}^2$; maximum coupling intensity up to $5.3\text{ W/cm}^2$).

### 2. Main Conclusions: What were the key dosimetry results?
* **Enhanced Peak Contrast:** Optimized the laser intensities and optical density to achieve a high-contrast Rydberg EIT peak height of 13% (at $51^\circ\text{C}$), which is more than double the maximum peak height previously achieved at room temperature.
* **Stable Linewidths:** Maintained stable spectral linewidths alongside the enhanced contrast, improving the overall sensitivity and performance of the quantum sensor.
* **Precise Magnetometry / Spectroscopy Data:** 
  * Measured energy level Zeeman shifts: 0.70 MHz/G, 0.93 MHz/G, and 1.12 MHz/G for the $|33D_{3/2}\rangle$ state, and 1.68 MHz/G for the $|33D_{5/2}\rangle$ state.
  * Measured a frequency difference of 336.4 MHz between the $|33D_{3/2}\rangle$ and $|33D_{5/2}\rangle$ EIT peaks.
* **Sensor Applicability:** Demonstrated that the real-time, high-contrast Rydberg EIT response enables the system to function effectively as a non-dissipative quantum sensor for traceable electromagnetic field detection and frequency locking.
