### 1. Core Method
The researchers addressed the challenge of mutual coupling in monostatic through-wall radar systems by replacing same-polarized (SP) antenna configurations with **orthogonally polarized (OP) Quadrifilar Helical Antennas (QHAs)**. By utilizing these orthogonally polarized antennas, the study aimed to isolate the receiver from the transmit signal, thereby mitigating receiver saturation and allowing for increased transmit power to better penetrate high-attenuation media like walls.

### 2. Main Conclusions
*   **Mutual Coupling Reduction:** The use of OP QHAs resulted in an **8 dB reduction** in mutual coupling at the 3.4 GHz operating frequency compared to SP configurations.
*   **Improved Detection:** The mitigation of receiver saturation successfully enabled the detection of weaker radar returns through high-attenuation obstacles.
*   **Performance Metrics:** Dosimetry and electromagnetic performance were validated through $S_{21}$ parameter measurements across the 3–4 GHz bandwidth, confirming the effectiveness of polarization diversity in enhancing system sensitivity.
