### 1. Core Method
The researchers developed and experimentally verified a **two-lens Huygens’ Metasurface (HMS) system** designed to expand the scan-angle range of a phased-array antenna. The system employs **wire-loop unit cells** characterized by high transmittance to manipulate the phase and wavefront of the radiated beam. The design was validated through full-wave simulations and physical testing conducted in an anechoic chamber at an operating frequency of 10 GHz.

### 2. Main Conclusions
*   **Scan-Angle Enhancement:** The system successfully doubled the scan-angle range of the phased array from $\pm 15^\circ$ to approximately $\pm 30^\circ$.
*   **Accuracy:** The system maintained high precision, with measured scan errors consistently below $2.9^\circ$ (specifically cited as approximately $\pm 2^\circ$).
*   **Performance Trade-offs:** The study confirmed a directivity degradation of 3.7–4.7 dB, which the authors noted is consistent with theoretical expectations for this specific two-lens architecture.
*   **Experimental Alignment:** The empirical results obtained from the anechoic chamber measurements showed a strong correlation with both full-wave simulations and theoretical design predictions.
