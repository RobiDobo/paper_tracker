Based on the technical details provided, here is the structured summary of the research:

### 1. Core Method
The researchers developed and utilized a **planar near-field measurement technique** to characterize the reflectance properties of five different millimeter-wave absorbers (AN-72, HR-10, LS-22, TK-S, and TK-L) in the **70–110 GHz** frequency range. Unlike traditional horn-to-horn measurements, this method captures the complex electric field on a 2D plane. By applying **Fourier transforms** to these field maps, the researchers are able to decompose the reflected energy into angular plane-wave components, allowing for the effective separation of **specular reflection** (mirror-like) and **diffuse scattering** (non-directional).

### 2. Main Conclusions
*   **Methodological Advantage:** The planar near-field approach provides higher precision, improved alignment tolerance, and lower sensitivity to standing waves compared to traditional measurement setups, making it highly effective for assessing stray light control.
*   **Material Performance:** There are significant differences in angular response and diffuse-to-specular ratios across the tested materials. Specifically, **HR-10 and TK-S** were identified as exhibiting significant diffuse reflection characteristics.
*   **Polarization Effects:** The data indicates that certain materials, particularly **HR-10 and TK-L**, have the potential to induce polarization conversion during the reflection process.
*   **Application:** These findings provide critical data for the design and performance assessment of high-precision cosmic microwave background (CMB) observation telescopes, where minimizing stray light is essential.
