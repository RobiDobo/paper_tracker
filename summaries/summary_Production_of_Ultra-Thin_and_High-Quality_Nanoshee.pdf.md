### 1. Core Method
The authors focused on the electrical characterization of solution-processed MoS₂ and silver (Ag) nanosheet networks. The methodology included:
*   **Electrical Characterization:** Utilization of dielectric and electrochemical gating, alongside the Transfer Length Method (TLM) and voltage-drop corrections to isolate intrinsic material properties from junction ($R_J$) and contact ($R_C$) resistances.
*   **Thickness Metrology:** A multi-modal approach combining Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM), optical profilometry (white light interferometry), and UV-visible absorbance spectroscopy. These were cross-validated against TEM cross-sectional imaging to ensure precise thickness measurements for subsequent conductivity and mobility calculations.

### 2. Main Conclusions
*   **High Performance:** The researchers achieved high network mobilities for MoS₂ (~30 cm²/Vs) and high conductivities for Ag nanosheets ($\sim 3 \times 10^7$ S/m).
*   **Junction Dominance:** The study confirmed that the performance of nanosheet networks is heavily governed by inter-nanosheet junction resistance. Minimizing this resistance is the critical factor required for the network performance to approach the intrinsic levels of individual nanosheets.
*   **Dosimetry:** There are no dosimetry results associated with this research. The provided text explicitly states that the paper does not contain information regarding radiation dose metrics, non-ionizing radiation safety, or human exposure.
