### 1. Core Method: What did they do?
* **Analytical Derivation:** The authors derived closed-form expressions (analytical solutions) for the Fraunhofer and Fresnel propagation boundaries specifically for phased array antennas (such as uniform linear arrays). 
* **Phase Analysis:** They evaluated the phase differences, maximum phase delays, and wavefront curvature across discrete element feeds—accounting for observation angles ($\theta$)—using binomial and Taylor series expansions, rather than relying on a single central reference point typical of single-element antennas.

### 2. Main Conclusions: What were the key dosimetry/propagation results?
* **Increased Boundaries:** Phased array antennas exhibit significantly increased Fraunhofer and Fresnel distances compared to single-element antennas of the identical total aperture dimension ($D$).
* **Fraunhofer Distance:** The maximum Fraunhofer distance for phased arrays experiences up to a fourfold increase, reaching $d_F = 8D^2\sin^2(\theta)/\lambda$ (or $d_F \approx 4 d_{F0}$) when operating off-boresight, compared to traditional single-element antennas.
* **Fresnel Distance:** The Fresnel distance for phased arrays was found to be $d_N = 1.75\sqrt[3]{D^3/\lambda}$, which is 8 times greater than its single-element counterpart.
