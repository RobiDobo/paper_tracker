### 1. Core Method
* **Exploitation of USB Power Management:** The authors developed **SPECTER**, a covert exfiltration channel that transmits data from air-gapped systems by manipulating host-side USB power management and device-state operations (such as VBUS power delivery and peripheral states via scripts and DevCon commands) instead of using conventional data transfers.
* **RF Monitoring Setup:** Near-field electromagnetic emissions generated by these USB power-control operations were captured using an **LF-R 400 loop antenna** placed in direct contact with the USB cable, connected to a **HackRF One software-defined radio** to monitor switching-related variations near **5 MHz**.
* **Signal Processing:** The RF receiver processing pipeline utilized a **short-time Fourier transform (STFT)** at a **2.6 MS/s sampling rate** (8192-sample segment length) to extract the magnitude of the zero-frequency bin ($Z(0, k)$), producing a dimensionless temporal trace ($R_k$) to quantify electromagnetic burst activity and verify transmitted bit sequences (e.g., 6-bit and 48-bit payloads).

* **Successful Covert Transmission:** The system successfully achieved data transmission rates ranging from **0.2 to 1.0 bit/s** across various host, smartphone, and peripheral configurations using state-based and event-based USB power control.
* **Qualitative RF Verification:** Electromagnetic activity—specifically switching-related bursts distinguishable from background noise near 5 MHz—successfully verified transmitted bit sequences. 
* **Absence of Biophysical Dosimetry:** The evaluation was strictly qualitative, demonstrating contact-probe signal reception without quantifying electric/magnetic field strengths, specific absorption rates (SAR), or biophysical results related to non-ionizing radiation dosimetry.
