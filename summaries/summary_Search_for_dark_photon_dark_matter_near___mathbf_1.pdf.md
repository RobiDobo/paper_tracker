Based on the technical details provided, here is the structured summary of the study:

### 1. Core Method
The researchers conducted a search for dark photon dark matter (DPDM) using a **cryogenic millimeter-wave receiver**. The experimental setup employed a superconducting **SIS (superconductor-insulator-superconductor) mixer** integrated with a **2SB (sideband-separating) filter**. The team performed a mass scan in the range of 1.0047578–1.0047598 meV. System characterization was achieved through electromagnetic simulation and physical calibration (using VDI measurements and hot/cold blackbody sources) to determine an effective aperture area of 18.4 mm², a system gain of approximately 60 dB, and a noise temperature of approximately 140 K.

### 2. Main Conclusions
*   **Detection Results:** No significant signal excess was observed during the search.
*   **Coupling Limits:** The study established 95% confidence level upper limits for the kinetic mixing coupling constant ($\chi$) in the range of **$(1.6–1.9) \times 10^{-9}$**.
*   **Systematic Integration:** Dosimetry-related data were successfully derived by incorporating total systematic uncertainty into the conversion power measurements to ensure the validity of the established upper limits.

### 3. Limitations / Future Work
The authors identified the following areas for enhancement to improve future sensitivity:
*   **Optical Upgrades:** Implementing parabolic focusing mirrors to increase the efficiency of photon collection.
*   **Instrumentation Upgrades:** Utilizing broader bandwidth spectrometers to cover a wider mass range of potential DPDM signals in subsequent experiments.
