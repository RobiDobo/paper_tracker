### 1. Core Method: What did they do?
* **Numerical & Mechanical Modeling:** Conducted 2D axisymmetric finite element method (FEM) numerical simulations using ANSYS Mechanical 2024 R1 to evaluate the mechanical behavior, von Mises stress ($\sigma_{\text{VM}}$), and normal stress ($\sigma_N$) distributions of stretch-free 3D island-bridge networks (featuring aluminum thin films and silicon islands). 
* **Parameter Variation:** Tested mechanical responses across bending radii ($R_{\text{bending}}$) ranging from 15 mm down to 1 mm (and down to 2 mm in electrical tests), while varying trench depths ($D$) between 10 µm and 20 µm. The study also incorporated mesh convergence analyses (evaluating element size and potential energy minimization) and material models with bilinear isotropic hardening.
* **Experimental Testing:** Performed experimental bending tests, scalability evaluations, and electrical characterizations (measuring threshold voltage, subthreshold swing, and saturation mobility of integrated TFT devices using a probe station) alongside fabrication steps like photolithography and wet/dry etching.

### 2. Main Conclusions: What were the key dosimetry/mechanical results?
*(Note: As the paper focuses on mechanical and electrical evaluation rather than radiation, "dosimetry" here refers to mechanical stress/strain dosimetry).*
* **Stress Localization:** Peak von Mises stresses localized predominantly at the bridge-island transitions, while a neutral plane was maintained at the mid-span. 
* **Stress Magnitudes:** $\sigma_{\text{VM}}$ increased as the bending radius decreased, reaching 115.8 MPa at $R = 1$ mm for a trench depth of $D = 10$ µm.
* **Structural Integrity & Plastic Deformation:** All evaluated stress levels remained significantly below the aluminum material's yield strength ($\sigma_{\text{ys}} = 545$ MPa) and critical plastic strain limits ($\varepsilon_{\text{pl}} \le 0.16\%$). 
* **Protection of Devices:** The stretch-free 3D architecture and silicon islands successfully acted as strain-free regions that protected the integrated thin-film transistor (TFT) devices from plastic deformation and irreversible mechanical damage under flexural loading.
