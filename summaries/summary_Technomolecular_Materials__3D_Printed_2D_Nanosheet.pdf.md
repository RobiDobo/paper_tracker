### 1. Core Method: What did they do?
* **In-Situ TEM Irradiation & EBL:** The researchers utilized high-energy electron-beam (e-beam) irradiation within a Transmission Electron Microscope (TEM) and via direct Electron-Beam Lithography (EBL) to examine beam-induced structural modifications.
* **Dosimetry Protocols:**

* **Materials Tested:** Saturated (C9/Ag) and conjugated aromatic (BPD/Ag, BDMT/Ag) technomolecular carbon/silver nanosheets.

### 2. Main Conclusions: What were the key dosimetry results?
* **Mechanisms of Interaction:** Electron-matter interactions induced electronic excitation, ionization, bond cleavage, and the reduction of $\text{Ag}^+$ ions, driving the nucleation of silver nanostructures via burst nucleation (LaMer mechanism), coalescence, and Ostwald ripening.
* **Dose-Dependent Structural Stability:** 
  * Saturated C9/Ag nanosheets formed homogeneously distributed encapsulated Ag nanoparticles (~10 nm), but eventually suffered significant charge buildup, structural tearing, and damage at high doses.
  * Conjugated aromatic nanosheets (BPD/Ag, BDMT/Ag) exhibited structural stability under high-dose e-beam mapping due to electron delocalization, cross-linking, and charge propagation.
* **Controlled Self-Patterning:** Combining high-dose patterning with low-dose secondary irradiation enabled precise control over dynamic diffusion aggregation (DDA) growth paths, successfully self-patterning metallic fractal nanoelectrodes (fractal dimension ~1.6) and demonstrating spatial redistribution of silver ions driven by damage-induced electric fields (DIEF).
