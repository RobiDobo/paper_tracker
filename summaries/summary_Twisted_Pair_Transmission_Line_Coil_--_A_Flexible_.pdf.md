### 1. Core Method
* **Simulation & Measurement:** Evaluated the radiofrequency (RF) and dosimetric performance of a novel twisted-pair transmission line coil (with varying twists per unit length, up to 64 twists) and compared it against a conventional copper loop coil.
* **Setup:** Utilized CST Studio Suite (frequency domain solver, tetrahedral meshing) with coils placed 2 cm above a homogeneous cubic phantom ($\epsilon_r = 67.9$, $\sigma = 0.48$ S/m), supplemented by 7T MRI measurements.
* **Evaluation Metrics:** Assessed $B_1^+$ transmit efficiency, 10-gram averaged maximum Specific Absorption Rate ($\text{maxSAR}_{10\text{g}}$) normalized to 1 W of accepted power, SAR efficiency ($B_1^+$ per square root of $\text{maxSAR}_{10\text{g}}$), inter-element decoupling, and performance stability under shape deformations.

### 2. Main Conclusions
* **SAR and Efficiency:** The twisted-pair configuration demonstrated comparable or slightly lower $B_1^+$ transmit efficiency than conventional loops, yielding a $\text{maxSAR}_{10\text{g}}$ of 1.0–1.1 W/kg at 5 cm depth (normalized to 1 W accepted power). 
* **Decoupling & Robustness:** The twisted-pair coils showed significantly enhanced self-decoupling ($\le -13$ dB) and exceptional robustness to shape deformations.
* **Operational Stability:** The coils maintained stable resonance and low inter-element coupling across various multi-element array layouts and physical deformations without requiring retuning.
