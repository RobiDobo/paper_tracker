### 1. Core Method
The researchers conducted a comprehensive characterization of charge transport in **graphene laminates** using a combination of experimental magnetotransport measurements and theoretical numerical modeling:
*   **Experimental:** They performed magnetotransport analysis (1.6 K–300 K; up to 13.5 T) and utilized material characterization techniques including **Raman spectroscopy** (to quantify defect density via $I_D/I_G$ ratios) and **XPS/HAXPES** (to verify chemical purity).
*   **Modeling:** They developed a **square-lattice network model** to simulate the charge transport, allowing them to decouple and extract individual nanosheet mobility ($\mu$) and interlayer tunneling conductance ($s$) from the bulk conductivity ($\sigma$).

### 2. Main Conclusions
*   **Dominant Scattering Mechanism:** Above 20 K, electron-phonon scattering was identified as the primary factor limiting electrical conductivity in the laminates.
*   **Vertical Transport:** Interlayer charge transport is governed by **phonon-assisted tunneling**. The research successfully linked electron-phonon coupling constants to the specific phonon modes regulating cross-interface transport.
*   **Doping Control:** The researchers demonstrated the ability to precisely tune charge carrier doping types through the thermal manipulation of intercalated molecules.
*   **Material Quality:** The laminates achieved high structural integrity, characterized by an exceptionally low defect density ($3.4 \pm 0.9 \times 10^9 \text{ cm}^{-2}$) and near-100% carbon purity after annealing.
*   **Magnetoresistance (MR):** The transition from quadratic to linear MR served as a signature of the interplay between intra-flake transport and inter-flake tunneling behavior.

### 3. Limitations / Future Work
*(Note: While the provided text focuses on findings, the following can be inferred as the inherent focus for subsequent research based on these results):*
*   **Scalability of Intercalation:** While thermal manipulation allows for doping control, further research is likely required to ensure the long-term stability and uniformity of these intercalants in large-scale applications.
*   **Interface Engineering:** Since vertical transport is limited by phonon-assisted tunneling at the interfaces, future work may explore surface functionalization or structural modifications to the nanosheet edges to further minimize resistance at flake junctions.
*   **Transport at Extremes:** The current study establishes the physics down to 1.6 K; exploring potential collective electronic phenomena or superconductivity at even lower temperatures or higher pressures could be a logical progression for this high-purity material.
