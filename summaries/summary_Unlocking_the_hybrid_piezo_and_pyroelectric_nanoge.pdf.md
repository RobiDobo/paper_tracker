### 1. Core Method: What did they do?
* **Device Fabrication:** Fabricated a flexible hybrid piezo/pyro-electric nanogenerator (PPNG) using vertically aligned $\mathrm{SiO_2}$ nanowires (NWs) grown via plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD), which were subsequently infiltrated with a poly(vinylidene fluoride) (PVDF) thin film and subjected to electrical poling (100–200 V) to align dipole moments.
* **Structural Characterization:** Utilized ATR-FTIR spectroscopy and nanoscale confinement/interfacial interactions (hydrogen bonding and electrostatic attraction between silanol groups and PVDF) to promote and quantify electroactive phases.
* **Electromechanical & Pyroelectric Testing:** Evaluated piezoelectric performance under mechanical cantilever deformation (10–12 Hz) and assessed pyroelectric responses by applying controlled temperature variations ($\Delta T$ of 9 to 22 K) using a hot air gun and a cold air stream. The pyroelectric coefficient ($p$) was calculated using the equation $I_{SC} = pA(dT/dt)$.

### 2. Main Conclusions: What were the key dosimetry results?
*(Note: As the provided text focuses strictly on electromechanical and pyroelectric characterization rather than electromagnetic radiation, "dosimetry" in this context refers to the quantitative characterization of electrical phase fractions and energy generation performance.)*
* **Electroactive Phase Enhancement:** Nanoscale confinement successfully yielded high fractions of the electroactive $\beta$-phase ($\sim 41\%$) and $\gamma$-phase ($\sim 59\%$).
* **Piezoelectric Performance:** The engineered $\mathrm{SiO_2}$ NWs@PVDF PPNG achieved a peak-to-peak open-circuit voltage of $\sim 3\text{ V}$ and a 9-fold amplification in instantaneous piezoelectric output power density (reaching a maximum of $10.8\text{ }\mu\text{W/m}^2$) compared to pristine PVDF thin film counterparts.
* **Pyroelectric Performance:** The poled system demonstrated a peak pyroelectric coefficient of $\sim 1.4\text{ }\mu\text{C/m}^2\cdot\text{K}$ at $\Delta T \sim 22\text{ K}$, representing a 4-fold enhancement over the poled pristine PVDF reference ($p \sim 0.35\text{ }\mu\text{C/m}^2\cdot\text{K}$).
