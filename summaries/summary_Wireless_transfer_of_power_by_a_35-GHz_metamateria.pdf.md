# Summary for Wireless_transfer_of_power_by_a_35-GHz_metamateria.pdf

Based on the extracted technical details from the paper, here is the final structured summary:

1. **Core Method**: 
   - Investigated the near-field behavior and wireless power transfer capabilities of a 35-GHz split-ring resonator (SRR) rectenna and a nine-element cascading array.
   - Utilized COMSOL finite-element simulations to model near-field horn antenna radiation.
   - Conducted experimental measurements of DC output voltage using microwave radiation between 20–40 GHz (at a maximum input power of 17 dBm), evaluating performance as a function of frequency, input power (within ICNIRP regulations), angular polarization, and distance from the horn antenna.

2. **Main Conclusions**: 
   - A single rectenna achieved a maximum measured DC output power of 50 µW and a peak power-to-DC conversion efficiency of roughly 1% at 3 mm from the horn aperture.
   - The system exhibited strong polarization dependence (aligned with the SRR gap) and distance-dependent voltage ripples indicating standing waves.
   - Integrating nine SRRs into an array successfully broadened the wireless power transfer response (21.7 to 30 GHz) and increased the DC output power by a factor of 20 compared to a single element.

3. **Limitations / Future Work**: 
   - Performance at high frequencies was fundamentally limited by diode threshold voltages.
