Main Conclusions** | • **Baseline SAR** – Textile‑antenna prototypes at 2.45 GHz exhibit SAR values ranging from **0.04 – 1.92 W/kg** (well below the IEC limit of **2 W/kg**, but highly geometry‑dependent).<br>• **EBG performance** – An EBG‑loaded design reduced SAR from **5.41 W/kg** down to **0.0545 W/kg** at 2.4 GHz – a **≈99 % reduction** (≈ one‑order‑of‑magnitude drop) while preserving radiation performance.<br>• **FSS / HIS / Metamaterial performance** – Implementations of Frequency‑Selective Surfaces, High‑Impedance Surfaces, and metamaterial unit‑cells achieved **up to ~95 % SAR reduction** relative to the un‑mitigated antenna.<br>• **Side‑band improvements** – The same structures simultaneously delivered **higher gain**, **greater radiation efficiency**, and **improved front‑to‑back ratio**, indicating that SAR mitigation does not come at the expense of antenna functionality.<br>• **Regulatory relevance** – All reported post‑reduction SAR values comfortably satisfy the IEC 2 W/kg (10‑g) exposure limit, confirming the practicality of the proposed solutions for wearable deployment. |

| **Take‑away for practitioners** | The reviewed surface‑wave‑suppressing structures (EBG, AMC, FSS, HIS, metamaterials) are proven, in simulation, to deliver **order‑of‑magnitude SAR reductions** while preserving (or even enhancing) antenna metrics.
